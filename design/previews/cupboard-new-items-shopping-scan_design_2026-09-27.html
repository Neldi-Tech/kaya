New Books & the Shopping Scan

Scan a book in the shop to know in one second if the family already owns it — then keep it (bought) or drop it (didn't). Bought books land in New Books, held by Mum & Dad, until a child asks and is given one.

Built on the real Cupboard code (Treasures 2.0) Reuses scan · lookup · dedupe (D29) Main direction + 2 alternatives

The real problem

Parents buy books the family already owns, and new books pile up with no clean way to hand the right one to the right child.

The insight

The scan already names and dedupes a book. We don't build a checker — we just move the check before the buy, and add one holding shelf between "bought" and "belongs to a kid".

Recommendation

Direction A. A "Shopping" scan mode → own/new verdict → keep or discard → New Books shelf → child requests, parent grants.

Prove it

Ship to one family for 2 weeks. Success = ≥1 duplicate caught in a shop, and ≥1 book requested & granted. Kill if the verdict feels slow or wrong.

Direction A — the Shopping Scan & New Books

the recommended end-to-end flow

The existing 📷 Scan to add sheet gains a first question: am I shopping, or shelving at home? In Shopping mode every scan is a verdict, not an add — "already yours" or "new" — with a keep / don't-keep decision. Kept books go to New Books (family-owned, parent-held). Children browse New Books and tap "Can I have this?"; the parent grants and the book becomes that child's. Nothing here breaks the current at-home add flow.

Alt B — Shopping Trip & child Wishlist

planned shopping, more power

Shopping becomes a saved Trip (a basket): scan many, each flagged own/new, decide later. Children keep a Wishlist; when a scan in the shop matches a child's wish, the verdict says "⭐ Ayan wished for this". At checkout you tick what you actually bought and it batches into New Books.

Why you'd pick this

  • + Wishlist is the magic. Children add books they want; scanning in a shop lights up their wishes — shopping becomes a treasure hunt for the right gift.
  • + Batch decisions. Better for a big planned trip — no per-book tap while you're browsing.
  • + Trip history: "what we bought in September".
  • – Two new concepts (Trip + Wishlist) on top of New Books — more to build, more to learn.
  • – Deferred decisions can leave stale trips lying around.
Jobs' take: The wishlist is genuinely delightful and worth building — but as phase 2. Ship A first (it already contains New Books + request/grant), then add Trip & Wishlist once the core habit sticks. Don't front-load complexity.

Alt C — Minimal: dedupe-first, no new mode

smallest change that answers the ask

No Shopping mode, no request loop. The existing scanner simply shows the own/new verdict the instant a scan resolves — before anything is added — and the tray's "already yours" state becomes the headline. Add one 🆕 New filter (family-owned, added recently, never read). "Can I have it?" is just a note to a parent, reusing the existing invite plumbing.

build order · definition of done

Build in this order (each step ships green on its own)

  1. Gateway check action (dry-run dedupe). Returns { lookup, onShelf }, writes nothing. Done: scanning a known barcode returns its shelf copy; an unknown one returns onShelf:null.
  2. Shopping mode in the scan sheet — mode toggle, verdict card (own/new), keep→add / discard→drop, trip tally. Done: a full trip can be walked with nothing added except the ones marked bought.
  3. New Books state + shelf band + 🆕 filter. Bought-in-shopping books carry the marker; a pinned band shows them; giving clears the marker. Done: a bought book appears in New Books and leaves it when given.
  4. Request → grant. Child request on a New Book; parent sees it in /parent/approvals; grant = give (owner→kid) or lend. Done: a child asks, a parent grants, the book becomes the child's; a gentle Sunday-meeting line optional.

Ship 1–3 to one family, watch a real shopping trip, then add 4. Alt B's Wishlist is the natural phase-2 delight once this habit holds. Everything rides the existing gateway → Flight Check clean on rules/indexes/storage.

Decisions only you can make

1. "Given" = keep or borrow? Default I've drawn: Give makes it the child's book; Lend keeps it a family book they're reading now. Keep both, or only one?

2. Who may keep books in New Books? Parents only (my default), or helpers too (the Cupboard already has a helper concept)?

3. Auto-shelve? Should a New Book that no child asks for after, say, 30 days quietly become a normal family book — or wait forever until you give it?

4. Scope now: A only, or A + start B's Wishlist? My recommendation: A now, B next.
